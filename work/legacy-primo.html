Silverglades

The Legacy Primo

A five-part social series for the top-end phase of an ultra-luxury residential project. It sells with facts instead of adjectives.

Client
Silverglades
Project
The Legacy Primo, Sector 63A, Gurugram
My role
Campaign concept, copywriting
Agency
Mediamix

The problem

Luxury property advertising tends to sell with superlatives, and the people who can buy a 4,800 sq ft home have read most of them. The Legacy Primo is the top-end phase of Silverglades' The Legacy, with 4BHK residences of 4,800 sq ft each. The series had to make it feel rare without sounding like everything else in the category.

The decision

Let the product make the claim.

Each post takes one verifiable feature of the home and states it plainly: the floor area, the number of homes on a floor, the open sides, the arrival, the clubhouse. The reader reaches the conclusion about exclusivity on their own. The copy never has to assert it.

The lines

  1. Only two homes share your floor.

    Scarcity, expressed as a floor plan. Privacy is never mentioned; the number does the work.

  2. Every square foot earns its place.

    Size is the obvious selling point, so the line turns it into a statement of intent: the space is designed, not simply large.

  3. Three side open residences that breathe.

    Light and air described through the architecture rather than through mood words.

  4. Exclusivity begins before your doorstep.

    The one abstract word in the series, anchored to something physical: the arrival.

  5. A private clubhouse with its own infinity edge pool.

    The amenity stated as possession. The weight sits on two words: its own.

The system

Every post uses the same three-tier headline: a handwritten lead word, the fact set large, and a line in capitals that completes the sentence. '4BHK | 4800 sq ft' sits in the same position on every frame, so each post returns to the product and the five read as a single campaign rather than five separate ads.

My role

Campaign concept and copywriting for the series.
